TRAVEL DIARY · KUWAIT

Kuwait City – Between a Bursting Bladder and Muslim Calls to Prayer

Less than 24 hours in an underrated country, an airport arrival that tested every last nerve, and a 25,000-step walk to one of Kuwait's most famous landmarks.

📍 Kuwait City, Kuwait 🌍 Less than 24 hours ✍️ Personal travel story
06

THE STORY BEGINS

Less than a day, more than enough drama

Kuwait was one of the few countries where I stayed overnight in a hotel, and the visit was part of one of the few longer holidays I've taken. You might therefore expect that I spent a reasonable amount of time exploring the country. Well, not quite. I spent just under 24 hours there in total, and somehow, that was more than enough time for a memorable collection of travel mishaps.

“

When your holiday starts with a delayed flight, an airport obstacle course and an urgent search for a toilet, things can only get interesting.

01

THE FLIGHT DRAMA

A flight schedule that couldn't make up its mind

The drama actually began several days before I even arrived in Kuwait. Before travelling there, I was visiting the United Arab Emirates. Even before landing in the UAE, however, I had already received countless emails from the airline operating my onward flight to Kuwait.

It all started while I was boarding the long-haul flight from my home country to the UAE. I received a notification informing me that my flight to Kuwait, scheduled for a few days later, had been pushed back by several hours. Instead of arriving at a reasonable time, I wouldn't land until after midnight.

With no internet access during the long flight, I had plenty of time to sit there and contemplate this delightful development. Fortunately, after arriving in the UAE, I found another email waiting for me. This time, the airline had moved my flight forward by around three hours. Instead of departing in the early evening, it would leave around midday.

As it turned out, this change was a blessing in disguise. My arrival in Kuwait would prove to be quite an adventure, and getting there earlier gave me a little more time to deal with everything that followed.

02

THE AIRPORT OBSTACLE COURSE

One urgent mission and an endless queue

The trouble began before I had even left the aircraft. I desperately needed to use the restroom, but the landing approach seemed to last forever. Naturally, finding a toilet was the first thing on my mind once we landed.

Unfortunately, Terminal 5 at Kuwait International Airport had other plans for me.

First, there was an interminably long queue at immigration. Travellers without a GCC passport, as well as those who had not previously entered the country, were directed to Kuwaiti immigration authorities for fingerprinting and facial scans. The procedure itself was understandable enough. The way it was handled, however, left a lot to be desired.

People pushed their way forward, officials chatted among themselves, and travellers were processed one by one. The whole thing took considerably longer than necessary.

After showing my passport and visa to what felt like countless officials, I was finally allowed to leave the terminal. You might think that was the end of the ordeal. Naturally, it wasn't.

My next question was how on earth I was supposed to get to the city centre and my hotel. I later learned that arranging a private taxi pickup at the airport is restricted under local rules. My apparent alternatives were a bus or an official airport taxi, but neither was easy to find at this small, chaotic arrivals terminal.

I don't usually accept rides from random people approaching me, but when a taxi driver offered to take me into the city, I was honestly relieved. He seemed friendly and helpful, and I didn't get the impression that he was trying to rip me off. More than anything, I was simply happy to finally get away from that airport.

✦

At that point, getting into a taxi felt less like a transport decision and more like an escape plan.

After a drive of around 30 minutes, I reached my hotel. Finding the entrance proved to be one final little challenge, but I eventually made it inside.

03

EXPLORING KUWAIT CITY

Mosques, cultural buildings and evening walks

I wasn't going to let the airport experience ruin the entire visit. Since it was already evening, the timing was actually perfect for exploring the city without having to deal with the scorching daytime temperatures.

Kuwait City turned out to be an interesting place to walk around. The city centre features mosques, cultural buildings and plenty of architecture to discover. There is also a famous traditional market that I would have liked to explore, although it appeared that the timing of my visit wasn't ideal for it.

Kuwait seemed to receive relatively few tourists, at least compared with some of the better-known destinations in the region. That made wandering around and getting my first impression of the country all the more interesting.

And then, of course, there were the calls to prayer. With mosques seemingly around every other corner, the sound became a regular part of the city's atmosphere throughout the day.

It was a different experience from the European cities I usually visit, and one that reminded me how much everyday life and the atmosphere of a destination can vary from country to country.

04

THE 25,000-STEP CHALLENGE

Walking all the way to Kuwait's famous water towers

The following morning, I decided to visit Kuwait City's world-famous water towers. Located along the coast at the northeastern edge of the city, they are among the country's best-known landmarks.

Getting there involved a rather long walk. But honestly, what are 25,000 steps when you're on holiday?

I had hoped to catch a glimpse of the Persian Gulf along the way. Unfortunately, the coastal promenade stretching from my hotel towards the water towers was completely blocked off by a construction hoarding several metres high. So much for a scenic seaside stroll!

Still, the walk was worth it. I had set out early, which meant that when I reached Kuwait's most famous landmark, I practically had the place to myself. I could take my time, enjoy the surroundings and appreciate the towers without crowds getting in the way.

And just like that, my time in Kuwait was almost over. Less than 24 hours after arriving, I was already preparing to leave the country.

05

THE NEXT DESTINATION

From Terminal 5 to a much better airport experience

After my morning walk, it was time to return to the hotel, check out and head back to the airport. This time, I was able to take the hotel shuttle, which was a far more relaxing experience than my arrival journey.

My flight to Oman departed from Terminal 4, which felt considerably more luxurious and better organised than Terminal 5.

After the chaos of immigration, the confusing transport situation and the seemingly endless queues, the contrast was hard to miss. At least my departure from Kuwait was much more straightforward than my arrival.

With that, my brief visit to Kuwait came to an end. It had been short, occasionally frustrating and certainly memorable — but I was glad I had made the trip.

MY FINAL THOUGHTS

Would I recommend Kuwait?

Absolutely! Despite my chaotic arrival and the frustrating airport experience, I genuinely think Kuwait is an underrated country that deserves more attention from travellers. There is more to discover than you might expect, from its cultural buildings and mosques to its famous water towers and traditional markets.

My main advice would be to plan your airport transfer in advance and, if possible, avoid arriving at Terminal 5 without knowing what to expect. My experience there made inefficiency feel like an art form.

And if you're not used to hearing the call to prayer throughout the day, be prepared: with mosques dotted all over the city, the sound is an unmistakable part of everyday life in Kuwait.

✦
THE ONE DAY EXPLORER VERDICT

An underrated destination with fascinating landmarks, a distinctive cultural atmosphere and an airport arrival I won't forget anytime soon. Kuwait is worth visiting — just give yourself enough time to get through Terminal 5!
