TRAVEL DIARY · ITALY

Venice – How Touristy Is It Really?

Rain instead of sunshine, a spontaneous trip to Bologna and an overnight train journey that left me wondering whether sleep was ever part of the plan.

📍 Venice, Italy 🌧️ March 2026 ✍️ Personal travel story
03

THE STORY BEGINS

One city, two night trains and very little sleep

Venice had never really been part of my plans. But when I discovered how cheap overnight train tickets could be, I didn't need much convincing. With the city's entry fees for day-trippers about to return, March 2026 seemed like the perfect opportunity to see Venice for myself.

The plan was simple: take a night train to Venice, explore the city for a few hours, make a spontaneous detour to Bologna in the afternoon and return home on another night train that evening. What could possibly go wrong?

“

I wanted to find out just how touristy Venice really is. I didn't expect the night trains to become almost as memorable as the city itself.

01

THE NIGHT TRAIN OUTBOUND

Cheap tickets, questionable sleep

This trip marked only my second-ever overnight train journey. And if there's one thing I've learned, it's that I'm not exactly a fan of sleeping on trains.

Between sharing a compartment with strangers and listening to the constant noise of a moving train, getting a decent night's sleep isn't exactly easy. That's why I always book a seat in the coach car rather than paying extra for a sleeping compartment. After all, if I'm unlikely to get much sleep anyway, why spend more money on it?

Surprisingly, the journey to Venice went relatively well. I was sharing the compartment with just one other woman, who was also trying to catch a few hours of sleep. With so few people around, I actually managed to get some rest.

It wasn't luxury, obviously, but at least I arrived without feeling completely exhausted. Little did I know that the return journey would be a very different story.

02

ARRIVING IN VENICE

Rain, wind and fewer tourists

Upon arriving in Venice, I wasn't greeted by glorious sunshine or beautiful blue skies. Instead, the weather served up rain and wind. Not exactly the postcard-perfect welcome you might imagine when thinking of Venice.

There was, however, one advantage: fewer tourists. And believe me, that makes a difference in a city like Venice.

The city is famous for its maze of canals, narrow alleyways, dead ends and countless bridges. It's the sort of place where you can easily find yourself wandering down a street only to discover that it leads nowhere.

Now imagine navigating all of that with huge crowds of people moving in every direction. Even with the relatively manageable crowds I encountered, I could easily picture how much more congested the streets would become during peak season.

So, here's my advice: if Venice is on your bucket list, consider visiting outside the busiest travel periods. Bad weather might not be ideal for sightseeing, but even on a rainy day, you'll find plenty of people who have made the trip.

✦

My Venice travel tip: fewer tourists are always welcome. A little rain might be a price worth paying.

03

A QUICK VISIT

Venice in a morning

I only spent the morning exploring Venice. Given the weather, the crowds and my plans for the rest of the day, that was enough time for me to get a first impression of the city.

I understand why Venice attracts so many visitors. Its canals, bridges and winding streets give it a character that is difficult to compare with other destinations. Simply walking around feels different from exploring an ordinary city.

Still, I wouldn't describe my own experience as breathtaking. Perhaps I had imagined something else, or perhaps the miserable weather simply didn't do the city any favours. Either way, I was glad to have finally seen Venice with my own eyes rather than relying on other people's pictures and opinions.

And that was only the first part of my day. In the afternoon, I spontaneously decided to head to Bologna for a few hours before returning to Venice later that evening.

But Bologna deserves a story of its own, so I'll save the details for my separate travel diary about that city.

04

THE RETURN JOURNEY

Waiting, exhaustion and a disappearing seat

After my spontaneous trip to Bologna, I headed back to Venice to catch the night train home. Anyone who knows me knows that waiting is not exactly my strong suit. Unfortunately, this journey gave my patience a proper workout.

The train didn't pull into the station until just before its scheduled departure time. It wasn't just me who found the late arrival surprising, either. Other passengers were clearly caught off guard as well.

By that point, I was exhausted and desperately looking forward to getting some sleep. After a full day of travelling and exploring, my own bed was beginning to sound like the greatest luxury in the world.

Unfortunately, my compartment had other plans. My cabin mate didn't seem particularly interested in sleeping and, to make matters worse, he was sitting in my seat.

At that stage, I honestly couldn't be bothered to make a fuss. There were only two of us in the compartment, so I figured we could manage somehow. It wasn't as though we were short of space.

That assumption didn't last long. Just as I was getting ready to lie down, two more men appeared. Apparently, they were supposed to share the compartment with us, too.

And just like that, any remaining hope of a peaceful night disappeared. Rather than attempting to sleep in an increasingly uncomfortable situation, I ended up staying awake for the rest of the journey.

By the time I finally got home, I was ready to collapse. And that's pretty much what I did — straight into my own bed, where I could finally sleep comfortably.

✦

Outbound: surprisingly successful sleep. Return journey: a seat dispute, unexpected roommates and an all-nighter. Consistency clearly isn't the railway's priority.

MY FINAL THOUGHTS

Is Venice really worth visiting?

To sum up my trip, I didn't find Venice breathtaking. I can absolutely understand why so many people are drawn to the city, and its unique layout certainly makes it an interesting place to explore. However, my own experience didn't quite live up to the expectations I might have had.

The weather probably played a part in that. Rain and wind aren't exactly the ideal ingredients for a memorable day of sightseeing. Even so, I was happy that I went. Sometimes, visiting a place yourself is the only way to find out whether it really lives up to its reputation.

As for the tourist crowds, my visit outside the peak season gave me a glimpse of what Venice can be like when things are a little quieter. I would definitely recommend avoiding the busiest periods if you prefer exploring without constantly navigating around other people.

Would I go back? Never say never. But for now, I'm glad I can finally answer the question that inspired this trip: just how touristy is Venice, really?

✦
THE ONE DAY EXPLORER VERDICT

A unique city worth seeing at least once, preferably outside peak season. My visit wasn't breathtaking, but it answered my questions — and gave me a night train story I'll certainly remember.
