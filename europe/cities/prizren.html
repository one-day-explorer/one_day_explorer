TRAVEL DIARY · KOSOVO

Prizren – I Did It the Way Others Did, and Because They Did

A spontaneous trip from Pristina, a bus ticketing system that initially made me feel like a criminal, and a fountain-water gamble that thankfully ended well.

📍 Prizren, Kosovo 🚌 Day trip from Pristina ✍️ Personal travel story
01

THE STORY BEGINS

When in doubt, do what everyone else does

Prizren was part of my surprise trip to Kosovo. If you want to know how that adventure began, you'll have to read my story about Pristina! While staying in the capital, I decided to visit the country's second-largest city as well.

Getting there sounded simple enough: take a bus and buy a ticket directly from the driver. What I hadn't anticipated was that figuring out how this system actually worked would involve a little observation, a lot of uncertainty, and a brief fear of accidentally travelling without paying.

“

No timetable, no driver in sight, no instructions. Sometimes, all you can do is watch what everyone else is doing and hope they're right.

01

THE BUS STATION

No driver, no timetable, no idea

Before setting off, I looked into how to get from Pristina, where I was staying, to Prizren. The answer was simple: take a bus. The catch? Tickets had to be purchased directly from the driver.

And there I stood at the bus station in Pristina, with no driver in sight.

Digital infrastructure was nowhere to be seen. There were no departure boards, no timetables, and no helpful displays telling me where to go. Instead, there was a bus with a scrap of paper in the windshield indicating its departure point and destination.

Eventually, I noticed several other travellers simply getting onto the bus without having bought a ticket. So I did the same. I climbed aboard and took a seat, hoping this was indeed how things were supposed to work.

Of course, I could have asked someone about the procedure. But finding somebody who spoke a language other than Albanian, the everyday language in Kosovo, would have been a stroke of pure luck. So instead, I sat down and waited for the bus to leave.

Sitting there without a ticket, I genuinely felt as though I were doing something illegal. There I was, on public transport, without anything to prove that I had paid. Except, of course, I hadn't actually had the chance to pay yet.

02

THE BUS EXPERIENCE

Cash, comfortable seats and a dental disaster

Eventually, the bus pulled away, and I finally discovered how the ticketing system worked. You wait until the bus is on its way, and then a conductor comes around to ask where you're headed.

You pay in cash, receive change if necessary, and that's it. No receipt, no printed ticket, and no physical proof that you've paid. The conductor simply keeps track of who has paid and who hasn't.

Once I understood the system, it all seemed perfectly reasonable. The buses themselves were surprisingly modern, equipped with air conditioning and reasonably comfortable seats. By Central European standards, there was very little to complain about.

Well, almost nothing.

The gaps between the seats could definitely have used a good cleaning. On the return trip, my dental retainer managed to fall straight into one of them.

Yes, my dental retainer. The thing that goes directly back into your mouth. It disappeared into a gap between the seats, leaving me with the sort of problem you really don't want to encounter on public transport.

Oops!

✦

The buses were modern and comfortable. The seat gaps, apparently, were a whole different story.

Apart from that little incident, the journey was quite relaxing. We passed through small villages, vineyards, and lovely natural scenery. It was a pleasant way to see a little more of the country along the way.

The only unusual thing was that passengers seemed to be able to get on or off almost anywhere, even in the middle of the main road. I, however, travelled from one terminus to the other, keeping things as straightforward as possible.

03

EXPLORING PRIZREN

A charming old town and a famous stone bridge

Upon arriving in Prizren, I headed towards the city centre, following a small stream until I reached one of its best-known landmarks: the old stone bridge.

Prizren is well known as a tourist destination in Kosovo, and it certainly lived up to its reputation. In the historic centre, one restaurant followed another, and there were people everywhere.

Despite the crowds, the city never felt uncomfortably packed. I could wander around, take in the historic buildings, and enjoy the atmosphere without feeling overwhelmed by the number of visitors.

The old town had a lovely character, with plenty to see along its streets. It was easy to understand why Prizren attracts so many visitors, especially those travelling from Pristina for the day.

At some point, while standing in the main square — or whatever the exact name of that particular open space was — I realised that I was almost out of drinking water.

Luckily, there was a fountain nearby.

There was just one small problem: I hadn't checked beforehand whether the tap water in Kosovo was actually safe to drink.

04

THE FOUNTAIN INCIDENT

Thirst versus the fear of a stomach bug

A quick Google search suggested that drinking the water was probably fine, although with one little caveat: proceed with caution.

Because nothing says reassurance quite like a search result that basically leaves you to make the decision yourself.

I noticed other people drinking from the fountain, so I followed their example and refilled my bottle. After all, if everyone else was doing it, how bad could it be? This was, once again, the exact same logic that had got me onto the bus without a ticket.

Unfortunately, the water tasted truly unpleasant. It was chalky and hard, and definitely not the refreshing drink I had been hoping for.

At that point, I was honestly more worried about an impending bout of diarrhoea than I was about dying of thirst. But with no supermarket in sight, my options were limited: risk a potential stomach bug or carry on without enough water.

I decided to keep drinking some of it anyway, simply to avoid dehydration. It wasn't exactly the most reassuring drinking experience, but what else was I supposed to do?

✦

The water tasted suspicious. My imagination was already preparing for the worst.

Fortunately, everything turned out fine. I was spared any unpleasant surprises and, most importantly, any additional trips to the restroom.

So, despite my questionable decision-making, the fountain incident ended without drama. At least my stomach decided not to join the adventure.

05

THE LEGEND OF PRIZREN

A city worth visiting — and perhaps returning to

All things considered, Prizren was well worth the visit. It has a charming historic centre, plenty of historic buildings, and lovely people. The atmosphere was lively without being overwhelming, making it a great place to explore at a relaxed pace.

The bus journey from Pristina was an experience in itself, but once I understood how the ticketing system worked, getting there was easy enough. And despite the dental retainer incident and my questionable fountain-water decision, the day turned out just fine.

But there's one more thing worth mentioning. According to a local legend, anyone who drinks from the magical fountain in the old town is destined to return to Prizren — and get married there.

Now, I did drink from a fountain. Whether it was the magical one is another question. And if it was, I suppose I may have accidentally signed myself up for a future wedding.

We shall see...

MY FINAL THOUGHTS

Would I recommend Prizren?

Absolutely! Prizren is a lovely city with a charming old town, historic architecture, and a welcoming atmosphere. If you're staying in Pristina, it's well worth making the trip to explore the country's second-largest city.

The bus system may seem confusing at first, especially when there are no timetables or obvious instructions, but observing what other passengers do helped me figure it out. Just keep some cash handy — and perhaps be careful where you put your dental retainer.

As for the fountain legend, I'll have to leave that one open. If I ever return to Prizren for a wedding, at least I'll know where the story began.

✦
THE ONE DAY EXPLORER VERDICT

A charming historic city, a relaxed bus journey, and a local legend that might just bring me back. Prizren is absolutely worth adding to a trip to Kosovo.
