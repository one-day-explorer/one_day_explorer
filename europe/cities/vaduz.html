TRAVEL DIARY · LIECHTENSTEIN

Vaduz – The Parking Lot Is Further Away Than the City Itself Is Large (But at Least It Was Free)

A six-hour drive, a tiny capital, a spontaneous border crossing, and a free parking space that turned out to be anything but a bargain.

📍 Vaduz, Liechtenstein 🚗 One-day road trip ✍️ Personal travel story
01

THE STORY BEGINS

A free parking space. What could possibly go wrong?

When I first started travelling, I often got around by car. One of my adventures was a day trip to Vaduz, driving all the way to Liechtenstein and back home again on the same day.

It began with a six-hour drive and a wake-up call before 3 a.m. For once, I had actually gone to bed early, yet somehow I still failed to get my eight hours of sleep. Nevertheless, the journey went surprisingly smoothly. Little did I know that the biggest challenge of the day would be getting back to my car.

“

The parking was free. The walk back to the car, however, came at a price.

01

THE JOURNEY

Six hours, one border, and a queue I could simply bypass

I drove through Germany and Austria, heading straight for the Liechtenstein border. Upon arrival, I found a long line of trucks backed up due to customs formalities. Liechtenstein isn't a member of the European Union, after all.

For me, however, it was no problem at all. Thanks to my EU passport and the freedom of movement within the Schengen Area, I was able to bypass the queue and enter the tiny principality without any checks.

And just like that, I was in Liechtenstein. No dramatic border-crossing experience, no lengthy wait, no paperwork. So far, so good.

At first, everything felt as though I'd landed in some small village: tiny settlements, a main road, and plenty of greenery. It was all rather peaceful. My actual destination, though, was the country's capital: Vaduz.

02

THE PARKING DECISION

Why pay for parking when you can park on a mountain?

Rather than parking in the centre of Vaduz, I chose a spot on a mountain road high above the town. The reason was simple: it was free of charge. And if there's one thing I appreciate on a road trip, it's not having to pay for parking.

The road itself was free to use, too, although "narrow" would be putting it mildly. In a few places, meeting oncoming traffic required a little more concentration than I'd ideally have liked.

But no matter! I left the car behind and headed downhill towards Vaduz. Along the way, I passed Vaduz Castle, the residence of the Princely Family and the country's most famous landmark.

I continued through the woods and was rewarded with a stunning view over the entire town. From up there, Vaduz looked absolutely lovely: green hills, picturesque scenery, and a peaceful little capital tucked into the landscape.

At this point, I was perfectly happy with my decision. The car was parked, the views were beautiful, and the day was going according to plan.

Please remember that feeling. It wouldn't last forever.

03

EXPLORING VADUZ

Luxury boutiques, a castle, and a spontaneous trip to Switzerland

Once I reached Vaduz, I explored the town on foot, passing the main square, the town hall, and countless small luxury boutiques. For such a tiny capital, parts of the town had a surprisingly elegant atmosphere.

I then continued towards the Rhine, which forms the national border between Liechtenstein and Switzerland. There, I found a small, old bridge crossing the river. It was a wonderfully simple way to step from one country into another.

Naturally, I crossed into Switzerland. Why not add another country to the day's itinerary when it's practically right there?

I followed the Rhine for a little while before returning to Liechtenstein via a newer bridge designed for vehicular traffic.

Finally, I made a quick detour to the Rheinpark Stadion, where Liechtenstein's national football team plays its home matches.

And with that, my sightseeing tour was essentially complete. I'd explored the capital, admired the views, crossed an international border on foot, and visited the national stadium.

Everything was going suspiciously well.

04

THE SURVIVAL CHALLENGE

The uphill walk that nearly ended my career as a tourist

Here's the thing about parking on a mountain road: walking downhill is wonderfully easy. Unfortunately, the car doesn't magically teleport to the bottom when you're finished exploring.

Eventually, I had to make my way back up that mountain. And when I say "up", I mean a genuinely steep incline that suddenly felt considerably less charming than it had earlier in the day.

It was the sweltering middle of the day. I had no drinking water. I'd been awake since before 3 a.m. And somewhere up that hill sat my lovely, conveniently parked, completely free-of-charge car.

So there I was, fighting my way uphill, questioning every decision that had led me to this particular parking space.

All that effort, only to drive back down the same road in my car a short while later.

✦

My travel tip: Free parking is great. Parking so far above your destination that retrieving your car becomes the main physical activity of the day? Not so great.

Admittedly, I was probably being more of a drama queen than necessary. I survived, after all. But I reserve the right to complain about a hill that could have been avoided by paying for a parking space closer to town.

05

THE UNEXPECTED TWIST

Five more metres, one municipal boundary, and a Luxembourgian encounter

Once I finally reached the car, I could have simply driven away and put the whole episode behind me.

But apparently, I wasn't quite finished.

For absolutely no practical reason whatsoever, I reversed another five metres out of the parking space, crossing the municipal boundary into Triesenberg before heading back downhill.

Because why leave a place without adding a completely unnecessary geographical achievement to the itinerary?

And then, just when I thought the day had run out of surprises, something genuinely exciting happened: I encountered oncoming traffic at the narrowest point of the road.

Some tourists from Luxembourg tried to squeeze their car past mine. We both inched forward, carefully manoeuvring between a sheer drop-off on one side and a stone wall on the other.

It was one of those moments when you suddenly become very interested in the exact width of your vehicle and the distance between your tyres and a potentially fatal drop.

Somehow, we made it past each other without incident. No scratches, no dramatic rescue operation, and no international diplomatic crisis between Liechtenstein and Luxembourg.

And that was that. My adventure was officially over.

06

THE JOURNEY HOME

Six hours back — and a whole new appreciation for my bed

With the sightseeing completed and the parking situation resolved, I headed back home.

There was just one minor detail: I'd been awake since before 3 a.m. and had already spent six hours driving to Liechtenstein. Naturally, the prospect of another long drive wasn't particularly appealing.

For a brief moment, I genuinely questioned whether attempting the journey home was a sensible idea. But I made it back safely.

After a day of castles, mountain views, international border crossings, and unnecessary uphill exercise, I was more than ready to call it a day.

At least I could finally go to bed without having to set an alarm for an ungodly hour.

MY FINAL THOUGHTS

Would I recommend Liechtenstein?

Absolutely — with a few realistic expectations.

My day trip to Vaduz was one of the most straightforward and hassle-free journeys I've ever taken. The drive went smoothly, the town was easy to explore, the scenery was beautiful, and I even managed to squeeze in a spontaneous visit to Switzerland.

Of course, many people will tell you that Liechtenstein is boring, tiny, and doesn't have all that much to offer. And to an extent, I'd have to agree. You really don't need more than a single day to get a feel for the place, especially if Vaduz is your main destination.

But that doesn't mean you should cross it off your bucket list entirely. Quite the opposite: Liechtenstein is peaceful, picturesque, and absolutely worth a visit. Sometimes a destination doesn't need a week of sightseeing or a packed itinerary to make for a lovely day out.

Just do yourself a favour and think twice before choosing a free parking space on top of a mountain.

✦
THE ONE DAY EXPLORER VERDICT

A lovely little country, a relaxed day trip, and beautiful views. Highly recommended — just make sure your parking spot doesn't become the main attraction.
