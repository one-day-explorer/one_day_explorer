TRAVEL DIARY · ROMANIA

Bucharest – Ghetto or Construction Site?

A cheap flight, a city full of construction sites, questionable infrastructure and a day that made going home feel like the ultimate luxury.

📍 Bucharest, Romania ☀️ Day trip ✍️ Personal travel story
01

THE STORY BEGINS

A cheap flight and absolutely no expectations

Some cities make it onto my travel list because I've dreamed of visiting them for years. Others simply happen to offer cheap flights. Bucharest belonged firmly to the second category. Being able to fly out early in the morning and return home late in the evening was an added bonus. What exactly was I expecting from Romania's capital? Honestly, I had no idea. But I certainly wasn't prepared for what awaited me.

“

When your expectations are already on the floor and a city somehow manages to disappoint you anyway.

01

THE ARRIVAL

Here we go again – starting at the airport

It all started at the airport. Again. Honestly, how could it have been any other way?

I landed at Bucharest Henri Coandă International Airport, also known as Otopeni, around midday. As I looked around, I was immediately struck by a familiar sense of unease. I'd experienced something similar when arriving in Kuwait.

But then again, so what? I hadn't come all this way to turn around immediately. Surely things would improve once I reached the city.

Unlike in Kuwait, Bucharest at least appeared to have a functioning train and bus connection to the city centre. I decided to take the train, as I generally find rail travel more comfortable. Simple enough, right?

Well, getting to the station turned out to be considerably more complicated than anticipated. Welcome to construction site number one.

02

CONSTRUCTION SITE NO. 1

A railway station with a rather questionable entrance

The station appeared to sit above an old, dilapidated parking garage that was undergoing renovation during my visit. The direct pedestrian route from the airport was therefore little more than a trail of rubble.

Instead of a straightforward walk to the station, passengers were directed along a makeshift path enclosed by construction fencing. It wasn't exactly the welcoming introduction to a capital city I'd been hoping for.

And the station itself? Let's just say it didn't improve my first impression. I don't want to be unnecessarily judgmental, but I genuinely can't remember seeing a railway station in such a neglected condition before.

Still, I wasn't going to let a questionable station ruin the entire day before it had even begun. At least the railway connection seemed to be working. A train was already waiting, and the announcements indicated that tickets could — and apparently should — be purchased on board.

This was despite two rather inconspicuous ticket machines standing beside the tracks. Naturally, I decided not to overthink the situation.

✦

The plan was simple: get on the train, reach the city centre and hope for the best. What could possibly go wrong?

I boarded and waited for the train to depart and the conductor to show up. Punctuality and cleanliness weren't exactly its strongest features. The train was extremely old and made a variety of strange noises, leaving me wondering whether it was simply part of its character or slowly falling apart.

Nevertheless, it did what it was supposed to do: it got me to the centre of Bucharest. I'll give it credit for that.

03

THE CITY CENTRE

Concrete, decay and yet another construction site

Unfortunately, arriving in the city centre didn't improve my mood. If anything, my already non-existent expectations sank even further.

Everywhere I looked, I saw old, worn-down concrete buildings. And, because apparently there hadn't been enough construction work already, another building site greeted me right outside the station.

The station itself was hardly a masterpiece of architecture, either. It looked like a bare, ageing building with no doors, surrounded by crowds of people. Among them were homeless people and beggars, adding to an atmosphere that I found difficult to warm to.

I began exploring the surrounding streets, hoping that the city would eventually reveal a more appealing side. And to be fair, it did. Bucharest has some genuinely impressive sculptures, beautiful churches and magnificent museums. There are buildings that make you stop, look up and appreciate the architecture.

The problem was everything in between.

Walking around required considerably more attention than I'd expected. Potholes appeared where you least wanted them, and loose or dangling power cables were an unsettling sight in several places. I found myself watching the pavement almost as carefully as the buildings around me.

And then there were the construction sites. They seemed practically endless. On one street, pipes were being replaced; on the next, the ground had been dug up; somewhere else, more fencing blocked the way.

Even one of the city's largest and most prominent squares had been torn up during my visit. At some point, I had to wonder whether I'd accidentally booked a sightseeing tour of Bucharest's entire infrastructure renovation programme.

✦

Bucharest's unofficial sightseeing package: historic buildings, uneven pavements and construction fencing in every direction.

04

THE REDEEMING FEATURES

Historic streets and two places worth visiting

Fortunately, Bucharest isn't all concrete, construction work and crumbling infrastructure. Eventually, I found the historic centre, where the atmosphere changed considerably.

This is where much of the city's tourist appeal seems to be concentrated, and I can understand why. The historic streets, cafés, restaurants and older buildings offer a much more inviting experience than some of the areas I'd crossed earlier in the day.

If you're visiting Bucharest, I would definitely recommend exploring the historic centre. It's worth seeing for yourself, even if the journey there leaves you questioning some of the city's infrastructure choices.

Another place that deserves a visit is the Palace of the Parliament. Located in the southwestern part of the city centre, this enormous building is hard to miss. Its scale alone makes it an impressive sight and a striking contrast to the surrounding urban landscape.

My second recommendation is the Patriarchal Cathedral, an important Romanian Orthodox church situated on a hill in the city. Its architecture and religious significance make it a worthwhile stop for anyone interested in history, culture or religious buildings.

There is, however, one thing I'd keep in mind when walking towards the cathedral. Along the roadside, I encountered numerous people asking for money, including beggars and small-scale vendors. Some situations were difficult to witness, but I personally chose to be cautious about handing out money to strangers.

Ultimately, these two landmarks and the historic centre were the highlights of my day. They reminded me that Bucharest does have places worth discovering, even if the rest of my experience wasn't exactly a love story.

05

THE COUNTDOWN TO HOME

When the airport becomes your happy place

As the day went on, I found myself looking forward to one thing more than anything else: going home.

That doesn't happen often on my travels. Usually, I'm already thinking about the next street to explore, the next landmark to visit or the next spontaneous trip I could squeeze into my calendar. In Bucharest, however, I was more than happy to call it a day.

I made my way back to the airport and went through security. Sitting there, waiting for my flight to board, I felt a sense of relief that was quite unusual for a trip that had lasted only a single day.

I wasn't thinking about all the places I still wanted to see. I wasn't regretting the time I hadn't spent exploring. I simply wanted to get on the plane and head home.

And when the airport departure lounge feels like the highlight of your destination, you know the trip hasn't quite gone to plan.

MY FINAL THOUGHTS

Would I recommend Bucharest?

Bucharest and I didn't exactly hit it off. While I appreciated the historic centre, the impressive Palace of the Parliament and the Patriarchal Cathedral, the overall experience left me disappointed.

The neglected-looking infrastructure, endless construction work and some of the conditions I encountered made exploring the city less enjoyable than I'd hoped. Of course, this was only my impression during a single visit, and a city undergoing renovation can look very different once the work is complete.

Perhaps Bucharest simply caught me on a bad day. Perhaps I chose the wrong route or failed to discover some of its better neighbourhoods. Either way, I didn't leave feeling that I needed to come back any time soon.

So, here's a final thought for anyone wondering whether Bucharest might be their kind of place: if you enjoy a more rustic urban atmosphere and don't mind rough edges, you may find things to appreciate here. If, like me, you prefer a city where the walk between attractions is part of the pleasure, your experience might be different.

For me, Bucharest wasn't the right fit. And that's fine — not every destination has to become a favourite.

✦
THE ONE DAY EXPLORER VERDICT

Impressive landmarks, a worthwhile historic centre and a whole lot of construction work. Bucharest had a few highlights, but I was more than ready to head home.
